Key takeaways

What the analysis found

  1. 1
    Career stats predict induction well.The models found 53 of 54 Hall of Famers among players they had never seen, and ranked every recent inductee near the top.
  2. 2
    Voters reward long, productive careers plus recognition.RBI, All-Star selections and wins carry the most weight. Reaching 300 wins or 3,000 hits is close to a guaranteed ticket in.
  3. 3
    The models can't see off-field factors or unusual roles.PEDs, gambling bans and scandals keep statistically qualified players out. Closers and defense-first players are underrated, and "very good" players are often overrated.
  4. 4
    24 strong candidates are coming.Near-locks include Albert Pujols, Miguel Cabrera, Mike Trout, Clayton Kershaw, Justin Verlander and Max Scherzer. On the current ballot, Carlos Beltrán is closest (70.3% in 2025).
Recommendation: Use the simpler logistic regression model with an 80% cutoff. Gradient boosting was not more accurate, and logistic regression is easier to explain. Expect roughly half of the players it flags to be inducted.

Limits to keep in mind

Why a model score is a guide, not a forecast

  • Off-field factors are invisible. PED links, gambling, scandals and reputation have kept statistically qualified players out. PED-linked and banned players were removed before training.
  • Some roles are misjudged. Closers (low win totals), defense-first players like Andruw Jones, and two-way star Shohei Ohtani don't fit career-total stats well.
  • Active careers are unfinished. Young stars such as Juan Soto and Ronald Acuña Jr. will likely rise as their totals grow.
  • "Not inducted" isn't always final. Committees still elect older players decades later, as with Dick Allen (2025) and Jeff Kent (2026).
  • Negro League stars were excluded. Their records are incomplete, so their stats understate how good they were.
  • Scores are rankings, not exact odds. In testing, about 56% of players scoring 80%+ were actually inducted.
Method: logistic regression and gradient boosting, trained separately for hitters and pitchers on players who retired by 1985, tested on players who retired 1986–2010. Eligibility: 10+ MLB seasons and 5+ years retired. Source notebook: Hall_of_Fame.ipynb.
